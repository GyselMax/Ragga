Why not rethink

The graph generator works and is deterministic and fast. CityConfig and the sealed quadtree records are good Java.

Why not continue as planned

There's no plan document, the work isn't safe, nothing is tested inside the build, and the next planned step (lots and zoning) repeats the old bet that generation can be finished before the economy exists.

This would move me to rethink

Even with defined money sources and sinks, a small plain-Java economy can't keep its money balanced and employment steady. Then “the city sustains itself” is out of reach at this scope, and the project should narrow to a generator plus a market model.

This would move me to continue

You decide that learning generation algorithms matters more than reaching the simulation. Then generation first is right, and the Executor's plan is the whole answer.

Next three actions

What moves the project forward this week

  1. Secure the work under an hour

    • Old repo: commit the untracked economy, company, owner, building and test files on a new branch (for example archive/grid-economy), and push only that branch.
    • New repo: before any commit, make the staged quadtree files match the working tree (git add the real files). Ignore graph.png and docs/.obsidian/.

    Done when the 57-test tree exists somewhere other than this disk, and git diff --cached shows your real quadtree.

  2. Build the safety net one evening

    • Move generation into a plain class that takes a CityConfig and returns the graph. GraphGenerator becomes a thin runner.
    • Decide on StrictMath first, since it can change the output (bump CityConfig.VERSION if it does).
    • Pin seed 67 in a JUnit test with no Spring: counts, an order-independent edge fingerprint, and a brute-force check of spacing, degree, angles and crossings. Move CityConfigTest into src/test.

    Done when ./mvnw test passes with MySQL stopped (remove or override contextLoads).

  3. Finish the quadtree yourself two evenings at most

    • Write the builder bodies by hand against a brute-force equivalence test. Use Q21 to check your answers, not as code to copy.
    • Wire the tree into the three scans. Then stop optimising.

    Done when the golden test from action 2 is still green with the tree wired in.

Should DESIGN.md or the roadmap change?

Yes: the rewrite needs its own design doc, in a new order

Write a README and a design doc for the rewrite (for example docs/DESIGN.md). Keep design-2.0.md as the history of the grid model rather than fixing it. The new doc should carry over the philosophy and the confirmed decisions, write down the exclusions (crime, health, diagonal roads) and the open tick-length question, and rank the three goals. The roadmap should put validation after the things it validates:

  1. Generation done

  2. Safety net and quadtree this week

    Actions 2–3 above.

  3. Plain-Java economy spike next

    Defined money sources and sinks: tax goes to a treasury, and an outside buyer takes industrial and farm output. Includes a money-conservation test.

  4. Lots and zoning

    Sized by what the spike needs: jobs and homes per lot.

  5. People, jobs and the tick loop

    Kept in memory, saved as snapshots.

  6. Minimal life events

    Hiring, layoffs, and deaths replaced by births.

  7. Player entry

Deferred

Counter-offers, homelessness penalties, personal bankruptcy, and the 3D engine.

Dissent kept

My call is the Executor's plan this week, then the economy spike before lots. The Executor would go from the quadtree straight to lots, and that's the better order if learning generation is your top goal.
