What each line does

An empty square over the map, then the starters go in

QuadTreeBuilder index = new QuadTreeBuilder(0, 0, Math.max(GRID_WIDTH, GRID_HEIGHT));

  • What it builds: the tree's top square, with its corner at (0, 0) and its side as long as the map's longer side: 1500 for you. It starts as one empty leaf.
  • Why Math.max: the tree is square, but the map doesn't have to be. isWithinBounds accepts anything in 0..GRID_WIDTH × 0..GRID_HEIGHT, so the square must reach the far edge in both directions. A node outside the square would be lost to every search.
  • Why here: it has to exist before the starters go in, and before the growth loop, which hands it on in step 4. It's a local in run(), like graph.
  • Why "index": it sits next to the graph like an index next to a database table. The graph answers "what is connected to what"; the index answers "what is near this point".

index.insert(seed);

  • What it does: drops the starter into the leaf that covers its position. With one starter, the tree is a single leaf holding one node. It splits into four when the 9th node arrives (CAPACITY is 8).
  • Why right after graph.addVertex(seed): the rule for the whole wiring is that every addVertex gets an insert. There are exactly two places: here for the starters, and in step 5 for every generated node.

  • In the node phase (step 5), isFarEnough only sees what the index returns. Candidates can then land within 40 of a starter, and every later random draw shifts, which gives a different city.
  • In the street phase (step 7), streets only step onto nodes the frozen tree returns. A starter that isn't in it gets at most the one street from its own turn, and none from anyone else.
